Research overview

How do the structures of learning environments shape understanding, participation, and knowledge construction?

My research examines how learning is shaped by the ways information is organized, collaborative activities are designed, and interactions develop. I study both the structures intentionally introduced through instructional design and the structures that emerge as learners engage with content and one another.

Drawing on learning sciences, instructional design, and learning analytics, I connect these educational questions with theoretically grounded analysis. My work spans individual knowledge structures, collaborative learning processes, and interpretable computational methods, with emerging interests in AI-mediated learning environments.

Core research areas

Understanding Learning Through Its Structures

1. External Representations and Knowledge Structures

How does the organization of information in instructional materials influence what learners understand and how they organize knowledge?

My early research investigated the relationship between external concept structures and learners’ internal knowledge structures. It examined how the organization of instructional information can shape the relationships learners construct among concepts. This work established the foundation for my broader interest in the relationship between designed learning structures and knowledge construction.

Selected publication
  • The influence of external concept structures on an individual’s knowledge structuresLee, M., & Clariana, R. B. (2022). Educational Technology Research and Development, 70(5), 1657–1674.

2. Collaborative Learning Design and Knowledge Construction

How do designed and emergent social structures influence learners’ participation, interaction, and knowledge construction?

I extend the study of knowledge structures to collaborative learning environments. My research examines group formation, network positions, collaborative roles, and the content of learners’ interactions. It connects the social organization of learning with the intellectual work learners perform together, including explanation, critique, and synthesis.

This work brings together studies of collaborative concept mapping, community detection, engagement in online discussions, and the design of role-structured discussion activities. A central concern is how findings about interaction can inform the design of collaborative learning environments.

Current research

From Collaborative Role Design to the Analysis of Learning Processes

My dissertation-based research examines predefined collaborative roles in asynchronous online discussions from three complementary perspectives: how interaction develops over time, how learners enact their assigned roles, and how theoretically grounded indicators of collaboration and knowledge construction can be identified computationally.

Temporal Development of Collaborative Interaction

In revision

Examines how participation and interpersonal interaction develop over time in role-assigned and unstructured groups, using temporal network analysis and complementary discourse analysis.

Lee, M., Sharma, P., & Clariana, R. When does structure matter? Social and cognitive outcomes in role-assigned versus organic online groups.

Role Enactment and Knowledge Construction

Under review

Investigates how learners enact predefined roles through knowledge-construction discourse and network positioning, linking the intended design of roles with observed patterns of interaction.

Lee, M., Sharma, P., & Clariana, R. Enacting predefined collaborative roles in asynchronous discussions: Knowledge-construction discourse and network positioning. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning.

Interpretable Text-Based Prediction

Minor revision submitted

Investigates the feasibility of identifying collaboration roles and knowledge-construction processes from discussion text using interpretable machine learning. The aim is to examine what computational prediction can reveal about educationally meaningful categories and where interpretation remains necessary.

Lee, M., Sharma, P., & Clariana, R. Text-based prediction of collaboration roles and knowledge construction processes in asynchronous discussions. Educational Technology Research and Development.

This research received the 2024 AERA Instructional Technology SIG Graduate Student Paper Award.

Cross-cutting approach

Making Learning Processes Analytically Visible

My methodological choices begin with the educational question and the constructs it requires. I combine evidence about who interacts with whom, what learners contribute, how interaction develops over time, and what these patterns mean in their instructional context.

Social Network and Temporal Analysis

Examines relational structures, group formation, network positioning, and changes in interaction over time.

Discourse and Content Analysis

Connects observed interaction with theoretically grounded categories of collaboration and knowledge construction.

Interpretable Machine Learning

Explores the feasibility of computationally identifying learning-relevant patterns and interpreting the evidence used by predictive models.

Mixed Methods and Evaluation

Combines quantitative and qualitative evidence to investigate learning experiences, evaluate interventions, and inform design decisions.

Across these approaches, I am interested in how computational scale and automation can support educational research while retaining attention to learning context, construct validity, and interpretability.

Emerging research directions

AI-Mediated Learning

Building on my research on external representations and collaborative knowledge construction, I am exploring how AI-mediated learning environments shape learners’ understanding, interaction, and knowledge construction. I am particularly interested in the implications of AI for instructional design and in developing theoretically grounded approaches to analyzing learning processes in these environments.

Applied and collaborative research

Extending Research Across Educational Contexts

Institutional Analytics and Student Success

As Director of Institutional Data Analytics at the University of Houston–Downtown, I investigated student progression, persistence, and academic support using longitudinal institutional data and interpretable predictive approaches. This work extends my interest in educationally meaningful analysis to student pathways and institution-level decision-making.

Current manuscript: Lee, M., & Tiwari, A. (Under consideration). Interpreting Academic Support Non-Use Across Academic Trajectories: First-Year Momentum and Institutional Attrition. Journal of Applied Research in Higher Education.
